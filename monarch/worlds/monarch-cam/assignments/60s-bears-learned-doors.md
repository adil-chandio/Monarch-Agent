## The promise

**Working title:** `Bears Have Learned How to Open Our Doors`

**Core promise:** Not random bear sightings. Show real bears exploiting human access points —
cars, cabins, handles, doors, windows and accessible food — with a visible escalation from
one scene to the next.

**Editorial question:** How did the bear get in — and what does its repeated behaviour tell
us about food conditioning and human access?

**Factuality guardrail:** "learned exactly" is the operator's proposed framing, not yet a
verified claim about an individual bear's intention. The final narration must describe what
is visible and distinguish observation from inference. Explain food-conditioning with a
citable, authoritative wildlife-agency source; do not infer a bear's thoughts from footage.

## Story escalation and timecoded structure

One coherent story, not a random bear montage:

```
Handle → Door → Vehicle → Bear inside → Bear family payoff
```

| Time | Required story beat | Edit law |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Open on the most unbelievable *verified* action: paw at a real handle, door opening, or bear visibly entering human space | proof on frame 1; no logo, title card, scene-setting, or waiting for the good bit |
| 0:03–0:10 | Introduce the question and premise. Working line: “This isn’t a random bear sighting. These bears have learned exactly where humans leave the easy doors.” | Natural English; revise any unsupported inference; VO must not delay visual proof |
| 0:10–0:25 | Escalate: testing multiple vehicle doors, checking another car, or a clear second access point | each cut adds information, tension, or surprise |
| 0:25–0:38 | Surprise: owner opens a car/truck and finds a bear already inside, or a bear successfully opens a real door/window | reaction must be authentic, not added or misrepresented |
| 0:38–0:52 | Biggest payoff: mother bear opens a car and cubs follow one by one, or a rights-cleared equivalent that is demonstrably bigger | the ending beat must top the opening; if not, reorder/re-source |
| 0:52–1:00 | Concise, sourced explanation: food conditioning can teach bears to revisit vehicles/buildings; exact next-video bridge: “Next: why bears keep coming back to the exact same homes.” | do not rush; if the evidence or time budget cannot support both, rewrite the narration while preserving an honest bridge |

The sample curiosity line and closing bridge are **working copy**, not mandatory claims. The
editor/scriptwriter may improve them for accuracy and natural speech; the factual meaning and
one-next-video conversion path must remain.

## Edit and retention targets

- Keep the actual animal/action visible and legible in vertical crop; never crop out a source
  watermark (drop the clip instead).
- 1080×1920, 30fps, 9:16; readable for TV and mobile; no tiny text or obstructive graphics.
- Visual refresh about every 2–5 seconds **when the story warrants it**; no arbitrary cuts
  that obscure what happened.
- A meaningful escalation/payoff every 8–15 seconds; strongest truthful visual appears first.
- Authentic raw sound / reaction where useful; subtle SFX and music below intelligible speech.
- No fake attack label, staged reaction, synthetic wildlife, graphic harm or countdown filler.
