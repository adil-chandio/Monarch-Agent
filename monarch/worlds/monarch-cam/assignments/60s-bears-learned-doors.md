## The promise

**Working title only:** `Bears Have Learned How to Open Our Doors`

This wording is **not approved**. “Learned,” “our,” a shared chronology, and a single bear may
all imply facts the current leads do not establish. Keep this title only as a searchable brief
label until actual footage and source/context research support or replace it.

**Core creative territory:** real bear encounters with human access points—cars, cabins,
porches, handles, windows, and accessible food. The finished piece must tell viewers which
moments are visibly shown, which clips are separate events, and what reliable sources say.
Do not suggest that multiple uploads show one bear, one location, or one continuous incident
unless the evidence confirms it.

**Editorial question (provisional):** What do the selected clips actually show about bears
approaching or entering human spaces, and what can reliable wildlife sources explain about
accessible food and bear behaviour?

**Factuality guardrail:** describe visible actions, not a bear's thoughts. Do not say “learned
exactly,” “knew,” “broke in,” “attack,” or claim a cause unless the actual clip and a reliable
source support that wording. If discussing food conditioning, use a citable wildlife-agency or
other authoritative source and distinguish general guidance from the cause of a specific
encounter.

## Story shape and timing — adapt only after scene selection

This is a **possible** sequence, not a factual chronology:

```
visible access attempt → a distinct example or escalation → verified outcome/reaction → sourced context
```

If scenes come from different uploads, animals, or locations, say/show that they are separate
clips. Do not edit them to imply one animal progressing through a chain of incidents.

| Time | Possible story beat | Edit / fact law |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Strongest *verified* visible action: paw on a real handle, door opening, or bear entering human space | proof in the opening; no misleading crop, logo, title card, or waiting for the real moment |
| 0:03–0:10 | Ask one honest question, such as “How did this bear get inside?” only if the selected clip supports it | use exact footage context; do not say “our doors” or claim one bear across clips |
| 0:10–0:25 | Add another attempt, a distinct clip, or a clear detail that changes the viewer's understanding | label separate incidents; each cut adds evidence, not just louder music |
| 0:25–0:38 | Show verified outcome or authentic source reaction | no added/misrepresented reaction and no invented before/after |
| 0:38–0:52 | Deliver the clearest payoff available in the approved set | “biggest” is an editorial judgement, not a requirement to escalate into danger or distress |
| 0:52–1:00 | Concise context from a reliable wildlife source + one relevant next-video bridge | do not turn a general food-conditioning fact into an unverified cause for this clip |

This table is a structure, not a finished script. If the evidence or time budget cannot support
the proposed explanation, simplify the narration while keeping the story accurate and the
conversion bridge honest.

## Edit and retention targets

- Keep the actual animal/action visible and legible in vertical crop; never crop out or hide a
  watermark. If the copy is not suitable, find an appropriate source master or choose another
  clip.
- Export target: 1080×1920, 30fps, 9:16 **when source quality and the actual editor support it**;
  do not describe an upscale as original-resolution footage.
- Readable for TV and mobile; no tiny text or obstructive graphics.
- Visual refresh about every 2–5 seconds **when the story warrants it**; no arbitrary cuts that
  obscure what happened.
- Use natural escalation and payoff; there is no mandatory “bigger ending” rule.
- Authentic raw sound/reaction where useful; subtle SFX and music below intelligible speech.
- No fake attack label, staged reaction, synthetic wildlife, graphic harm, or countdown filler.
