# Learning design, curiosity and audio — evidence with boundaries

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-06 · Research supports design choices in particular contexts; it does not promise views, retention or control over a viewer.

## Educational/explainer visual design

For instructional material, multimedia-learning research supports testing these principles: remove material irrelevant to the learning goal (coherence), signal important structure, keep the visual close to the narration that explains it, and organize complex material into meaningful segments. These are learning-design findings; effects vary by learner, prior knowledge, material and context. They are not a universal rule for entertainment editing or a YouTube-ranking formula. [Mayer & Fiorella, *The Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning*, review chapter](https://edtechuvic.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/09/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles.pdf)

Practical reviewer question: **does this visual, sound or line help the viewer understand this beat, feel a justified emotion, or follow the story?** If not, consider removing it. In Style B, the user's one-cluster-per-scene, maximum-three-colour, character-palette, safe-area, contrast, one-transition and pixel-evidence laws remain the controlling design contract. Read the existing Style-B skill/reference rather than creating a competing summary.

## Curiosity: honest gap, real payoff

Information-gap theory is a useful story hypothesis: a question can invite information seeking when the audience understands what is missing and believes the answer is worth learning. In a 2026 article-reading study, two preregistered experiments (n=501 and n=511) found that making moderate knowledge gaps salient increased chapter-reading/information seeking; one experiment also reported greater knowledge gains. The task was reading about elephant conservation, not watching a video. Use it to motivate a test of clear questions plus credible payoff—not as proof of a retention or virality recipe. [Frede et al., *Journal of Cognition* (2026)](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/10.5334/joc.501)

Never create a fake gap, tease an answer the video cannot deliver, or use anxiety/deception merely to extend watch time. Maintain the existing Monarch promise/payoff and approval gates.

## Neuroscience claims

Dopamine is not a one-line “attention button.” A 2024 *Nature Neuroscience* perspective describes important evidence and unresolved complexity beyond a simple reward-prediction-error account. Do not tell viewers or train agents that a script, sound cue or thumbnail “hacks dopamine” or controls a viewer's brain. If a production makes a neuroscience claim, source the exact claim and describe uncertainty accurately. [Gershman et al. (2024)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-024-01705-4)

## Loudness and ASMR

EBU R 128 version 5 recommends an average programme loudness of -23 LUFS and Loudness Range / Maximum True Peak descriptors for a broadcast context. That is not a universal YouTube target. The active approved delivery spec and the existing Style-B technical contract govern the project; do not silently swap them for a broadcast number. [EBU R 128 v5 (2023)](https://tech.ebu.ch/publications/r128)

For Monarch's ASMR work, preserve the user's contract: stereo width is required; keep VO centered, keep the bed wide, and meter VO and bed from separate stems. Do not certify pleasantness, spatial stability, or ASMR quality from a scalar meter alone. Run objective checks on the delivered file and escalate genuinely subjective or unavailable evidence honestly.
